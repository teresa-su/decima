    pseudo_mag_inflate: widening the pseudo absolute magnitude error for reddened stars¶

    On by default. Config(pseudo_mag_inflate=False) turns it off.

    The problem¶

    Stage 1 conditions the main NPE on the pseudo absolute magnitude

    M_pseudo,K = ϖ · 10^(0.2 Ks − 2) = 10^(−0.2 (M_Ks − A_Ks))

    The NPE was trained without extinction, so it reads M_pseudo,K as if A_Ks = 0. For a reddened star, M_pseudo,K is then 10^(0.2 A_Ks) times too large, and the star looks more luminous than it is.

    Stage 2 fits the extinction and would correct this, but only by reweighting the stage-1 draws. If stage 1's proposal is centred on the wrong luminosity and is narrow, few draws land where stage 2 wants them. Stage 2 then rests on a handful of draws: low ess_frac, and noisy, sometimes biased results.

    What pseudo_mag_inflate does¶

    1. Estimate A_K cheaply from data already in the input.
    2. The Casagrande+21 colour–T_eff relation for Gaia EDR3 BP and 2MASS Ks (their Table A1, (BP−Ks)₀) gives T_eff = f((BP−Ks)₀, log g, [Fe/H]).
    3. Inverting it at the star's spectroscopic T_eff, log g and [Fe/H] gives the intrinsic colour (BP−Ks)₀, so E(BP−Ks) = (BP−Ks)_obs − (BP−Ks)₀.
    4. Then A_K = 0.124 · E(BP−Ks), clipped at 0.
    5. This needs no dust map and no external E(B−V).

    6. Widen, don't correct. The M_pseudo,K error is increased in quadrature by the fraction of M_pseudo,K the extinction could account for:

      σ² → σ² + (0.2 ln 10 · M_pseudo,K · A_K)²

    The value of M_pseudo,K is left alone. Stage 2 still does the actual extinction fit, with all bands. The inflation only makes the stage-1 proposal wide enough for stage 2 to work with.

    The estimate is noisy: σ(A_K) ≈ 0.006 mag from star to star on Xiang+24. That is small next to the A_K of the stars where the inflation matters, but not next to the median (≈0.017). This noise is why the estimate is used only to widen the error, never to correct M_pseudo,K.

    When it applies¶

    A star's error is inflated (pseudo_mag_inflated = True in the summary) only when all of these hold: - BP and Ks are both present. - BP > 5 and G > 6 (brighter stars are saturated in Gaia). - The relation has a solution with (BP−Ks)₀ in [0.30, 4.20] that reproduces the star's T_eff to within 1 K. In practice this means 4000 K ≲ T_eff ≲ 8000 K, the calibrated range.

    Otherwise the error is left as it was, and the star runs normally.

    Reference¶

    Casagrande, L. et al. 2021, MNRAS 507, 2684: The GALAH survey: effective temperature calibration from the InfraRed Flux Method in the Gaia system.
